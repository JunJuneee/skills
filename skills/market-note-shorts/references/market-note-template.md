# Market Note template and content rules

This is the retained house style for daily Korea and U.S. market-close cards. It is a decision guide, not a requirement to force every fact into every episode.

## Shared visual template

- Use the same calm card system for Korea and U.S. episodes so a viewer recognizes the series immediately.
- Use a 1080×1920 canvas. In the social-safe version, important text begins around x=84 and ends no later than about x=864. Keep equal left and right outer margins and leave the lower-right action rail free of essential text.
- Prefer large mobile type: headlines around 86–110 px, card labels around 31–45 px, primary values around 47–62 px, and explanatory copy around 39–42 px. Reduce copy before shrinking type.
- Cards align to shared guides and use consistent rounded corners, translucent white fill, and soft shadow. Paired cards must have equal heights.
- The standard header may show the date and `Market Note`; the social-safe render hides the header, progress bar, and source footer because platform UI occupies those areas.

## Korea episode content

The usual evidence chain is:

1. KOSPI and KOSDAQ close and direction.
2. Foreign, institutional, and retail flows.
3. Semiconductor leadership or weakness, especially Samsung Electronics and SK hynix.
4. KOSDAQ themes such as secondary batteries, biotech, materials, parts, or equipment.
5. Breadth or a large-cap/individual-stock contrast.
6. Movers ordered exactly as shown and narrated.
7. Two next-session checks, such as large-cap flow stabilization and KOSDAQ rotation.

If the index and breadth disagree, explain the divergence rather than hiding it. A few high-weight stocks can drag an index down while many individual names rise.

## U.S. episode content

The usual evidence chain is:

1. S&P 500, Nasdaq, Dow, and Russell 2000.
2. A grouped index sentence rather than four repetitive sentences.
3. Rates, oil, inflation, jobs, or another verified macro driver.
4. A sector or earnings contrast.
5. Russell divergence when it is meaningful.
6. Movers in visual order.
7. What to watch next session or next week.

When rates or oil is the driver, say how it changes the market interpretation. When an earnings beat is accompanied by a weaker outlook, explain both pieces instead of calling it simply “good” or “bad.”

## Display versus TTS

Always deliver two scripts. Display copy uses normal spelling and notation. TTS copy uses the pronunciation spellings in [script-style.md](script-style.md). Never place pronunciation spellings on the cards.

The TTS script should be one sentence per line, use periods for sentence endings, avoid ordinary commas, and omit the disclaimer narration. It should be rich enough that each visual card has time to remain on screen; do not add decorative cards that the narration does not support.

## Approval and handoff

For an image/script request, render and show cards first. Ask the user to generate or provide the complete ElevenLabs MP3. After the MP3 arrives, analyze it, place cuts at actual speech boundaries, render the video, validate duration and codec, and then archive it. If the user requests YouTube or Instagram publication, treat that as a separate external action requiring the relevant publishing skill and explicit privacy approval.
